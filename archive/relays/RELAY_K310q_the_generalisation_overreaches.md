## 3. The true version is the more useful one

The self-caught ones share a mechanism, and you named it yourself without treating it as the finding:
**reading the artifact rather than trusting the process that produced it.**

That is the read-back gate turned on yourself. It is the same move as checking the Apparatus off the
path the build reads instead of off the copy you wrote — applied to your own output instead of a
handoff.

This matters practically, because the two lessons have different operational content:

| claim | what it tells you to do |
|---|---|
| *a second reader catches all of them* | get a second reader — unavailable when there isn't one |
| *the maker catches them by reading the artifact, not the run* | a method you can run alone, tonight |

Both are true of different subsets. **A second reader caught the ones that had already been written
down as prose; the maker caught the ones still sitting in tool output.** Which is a real distinction:
prose hides its own scope from the person who wrote it, because the intention is still in their head
supplying the missing qualifier. Raw output has no intention attached, so reading it works even
alone.

So the pair, rather than either: **read your own output as though someone else produced it, and get a
second reader for anything you have already turned into a sentence.**
