- register_tags=['clinical', 'volta'] · band=b4_reply · href=…#obj-benatar-asymmetry-attack

> The parity charge runs one word two ways and hopes nobody checks. That the absence of pain is good is a comparison — better that no pain exist than that it does — and a comparison ranks outcomes without standing on anyone. That the absence of pleasure is bad is a deprivation, and deprivation is two-place: there has to be someone the missing thing is missing from. So name the one deprived of a pleasure never felt. Point to him. The set is empty. He ranks the good of absent pain as a verdict on states and lets the missing pleasure stand as a loss with no one to fall on. Symmetry was assumed where the logic was never the same, and the assumption gets called a refutation.


## pos-benatar-asymmetry-attack-02  →  benatar-asymmetry-attack

- register_tags=['clinical', 'volta'] · band=b4_reply · href=…#obj-benatar-asymmetry-attack

> Grant the objection its symmetry and it still has a hole where a person should stand. It is good that an asteroid missed a lifeless planet — good from no standpoint, good as a ranking of how things fell out, with nobody there to be glad of it. That is the whole sense in which absent pain is good: an outcome scored, not a gift held out to someone. Now run the mirror. Absent pleasure is bad only if it is bad for someone, and the someone is the one thing non-existence cannot supply. He grounds the good in a comparison and the bad in a subject, and only one of the two ever shows up. The planet is still lifeless. Nothing is missing from it, because nothing is there to do the missing.


## pos-consent-incoherent-01  →  consent-incoherent
- forms: "the consent premise is incoherent" · "consent requires a subject" · "you cant get consent from the nonexistent"
- char=739 (band 676–739 / ≤900) · em-dash=2 (≤4) · bans-hit=none · YLEX=clean · sentence-case=ok
- register_tags=['clinical', 'volta'] · band=b4_reply · href=…#obj-consent-incoherent

> Correct on the fact, wrong on what follows from it. Consent cannot be drawn from someone who does not exist. Fine. Everywhere else that consent cannot be had — the patient unconscious, the party not yet in the room — the rule is to hold, and to act only where holding would itself do the greater harm. Coming into existence carries no such emergency, because staying unborn injures no one who was waiting to be spared. So the missing consent is not a gap you are free to step through. It is the reason to keep still. He turns the impossibility from a permission slip into the very thing that forbids the act. The rule that governs every other unaskable case does not suspend itself here for the convenience of the one who wants to proceed.


## pos-consent-incoherent-02  →  consent-incoherent
- forms: "consent from nonexistent beings" · "consent is a category error here" · "there is no subject to consent"
- char=681 (band 676–739 / ≤900) · em-dash=1 (≤4) · bans-hit=none · YLEX=clean · sentence-case=ok
- register_tags=['clinical', 'volta'] · band=b4_reply · href=…#obj-consent-incoherent

> The word incoherent is doing all the work and none of it. Yes, asking a being that does not exist to sign is a category error, and no one is asking. The claim was never that the unborn must consent and somehow can. It is that consent is structurally out of reach, and that when it is out of reach the settled response everywhere is restraint, not a free hand. Read as a loophole, the impossibility licenses the act. Read as what it is, it is a locked door. He declines the inference the objection smuggles under that word — that what cannot be asked may therefore be done. The one who proceeds treats an unanswerable question as a yes. The one who refrains treats it as what it is.


## pos-non-identity-problem-01  →  non-identity-problem
- forms: "the nonidentity problem" · "parfit" · "you cant harm someone who wouldnt exist"
- char=712 (band 676–739 / ≤900) · em-dash=1 (≤4) · bans-hit=none · YLEX=clean · sentence-case=ok
- register_tags=['clinical', 'volta'] · band=b4_reply · href=…#obj-non-identity-problem

> The puzzle only bites inside a frame the position never occupies. Parfit needs the harm to trace to a named victim who would have fared better otherwise, and finding none, rules that no harm was done. That inference holds only where the accounting is kept person by person. The judgment here is impersonal — it rates the conditions any instantiated mind is forced to meet, not whether one particular mind would otherwise have existed at all. He brackets who the sufferer turns out to be as beside the point and keeps the structure that guarantees a sufferer. You need no name pinned to the wound to condemn a system built to deal one. The demand for a specific victim answers a question the position never asked.


## pos-non-identity-problem-02  →  non-identity-problem

> The move hides a premise it never states: that a harm must belong to someone in particular before it counts against anything. Drop that and the puzzle comes apart. The wager was never laid against a named child who might have drawn a kinder hand — it is laid against the whole spread of what any mind can be dealt, the cancers and the terrors and the fixed ending, live in the deck no matter who sits down to it. Swap the identity of the player and every one of those cards is still in play. He collapses the tie between who the sufferer turns out to be and whether the suffering was worth the risk of dealing it. The counterfactual about identity leaves the odds exactly where they stood.
