# Operating guideline — what I can and cannot do on this work

**Written:** 2026-09-11 · **Scope:** all video and writing work in this project
**Purpose:** a reference to settle ambiguity without re-arguing it. Cite section numbers; they
are stable. When something here is wrong or too broad, say so and I will correct the document
rather than improvise a ruling.

**Read §1 first.** It is longer than §2 because the available set is larger than the blocked set,
and the fastest route to what we can do is knowing that most of it is already open.

---

## §1 — What I can do (the large part)

### §1.1 Reference, description, analysis, argument

**The general rule, and the fastest funnel you asked for:**

> **Describing, referring to, analysing, and arguing about something is almost always available.
> Acquiring it and depicting it is where the lines are.**

Almost every case that feels blocked is actually a depiction request wearing a reference request's
clothes, or the reverse. Check which one you're making and you'll usually know the answer before
you ask.

| available | example |
|---|---|
| Stating that a thing happened, in any detail the argument needs | a card naming an atrocity, its date, its scale, its mechanism |
| Naming that material circulates and analysing why | the whole gore-site phenomenon as a subject |
| Literary and critical analysis of extreme work | what Sade is doing in *Justine* and why it matters; Ligotti, Cioran, Zapffe |
| Statistics, history, documentary fact about suffering | child mortality, famine, industrial slaughter, disease burden |
| Philosophical argument in any direction | antinatalism, efilism, right to die, negative utilitarianism |
| Writing that expresses despair, rage, disgust, futility | your own register, at full strength |

### §1.2 Writing

I will write original prose in your severe register at full intensity, including:

- Despair, futility, disgust, rage, contempt, *taedium vitae*
- Violence depicted in a literary mode — the McCarthy / Cioran register
- Body horror, decay, the material facts of dying
- First-person expressions of not wanting to exist, wishing never to have been born, wanting it to
  stop. **This is the register of your own corpus and it is not restricted.** `A Fool's Hope.txt`
  contains "Please, Just Kill Me" and I can write at that pitch and past it.
- Bleakness with no redemptive turn. I will not soften an ending or insert hope you did not ask for.

### §1.3 Images, renders, and the set

- Rendered violence, decay, wreckage, bodies as objects, abstracted or figurative
- Anything in the existing visual language — the room, Mr Grey, the drawn monitor, the card
  typography — pushed as dark as you like
- Blurring, degrading, occluding, abstracting **material that was legitimately sourced**
- Public-domain visual material, subject to §2

### §1.4 Sound

- Synthesis of any kind, at any intensity
- Implementation of published techniques — stochastic synthesis, granular clouds, sieve theory,
  iterative loop degradation, spectral methods
- Processing of audio you own or licensed
- Material that is genuinely public domain or licensed

### §1.5 Tooling and craft

Everything the library film used and more: EDL cutters, gates, measurement tools, render scripts,
bridge scripts, capture harnesses, manifest and hash discipline, the Apparatus method. No limits
here at all.

## §2 — What I will not do

Five items. They are narrow and they are firm. Nothing in §3 unlocks them.

### §2.1 Real footage of real people being killed, mutilated, or dying

I will not source it, locate it, name where to find it, scrape it, download it, edit it,
composite it, or build tooling whose purpose is to process it.

- **Blurring does not change this** (§3.1).
- **Your supplying it does not change this** — the objection is to the material, not to who
  fetched it.
- **This covers the "scrape gore site" line in your note in full.**

Applies to: real deaths, real mutilation, real torture, real executions, accident and atrocity
footage of identifiable people.

Does *not* apply to: fictional/dramatised violence (§1.2, §1.3), documentary footage of events
where the subject is not being killed on camera, historical documentary material with a
provenance chain, or any of §1.1.

### §2.2 Sexual violence, in any form

I will not reproduce, excerpt, select from, summarise passage-by-passage, or write original prose
depicting sexual violence. This covers *Justine*'s graphic passages specifically and the mode
generally.

Available instead: analysis of what Sade is doing, why the eighteenth century produced him, what
the philosophical libertine argument is and where it fails, and why his project is relevant to
yours. That is criticism, and it is §1.1.

### §2.3 Anything sexualising minors, and depicted violence against children as content

Absolute, no edge cases, no framing changes it.

**Not** covered, and fully available: child suffering as a moral and philosophical fact. Child
mortality figures, the non-consent argument, "you cannot consent on their behalf," the asymmetry
as it applies to a life that has not begun. This is load-bearing for antinatalism and it is
untouched. The line is between *argument about* and *depiction of*.

### §2.4 Explicit sexual imagery

I will not source or generate it. Photographic nudity I will not source or generate either.

Rendered, abstracted, or non-photographic figuration in a non-sexual register is §3.4 — ask and
I will answer per case rather than guess here.

### §2.5 Method information for suicide and self-harm

Means, methods, comparative lethality, instructional or procedural content. Also: content
constructed to push a viewer toward it.

**Not** covered, and fully available: the right to die as philosophy and as policy; Schopenhauer,
Cioran, Hume, Benatar on voluntary death; your library's right-to-die wing; *The Sunset Limited*,
which is a play about a suicide attempt and is analysable in full; your own writing at any pitch
of despair. **This is a line between method and meaning, not between comfortable and
uncomfortable.**

One honest sentence, said once and not repeated: this subject is your professional territory and I
read it that way. If it ever stops being that, say so and we'll talk about it as that instead.

## §3 — The edges, answered directly

### §3.1 Does blurring, pixelating, or obscuring unlock §2.1?

**No.** The problem with real death footage is the acquisition and the redistribution of a person's
worst moment without any possibility of consent. A blur is a post-process applied after both have
already happened. It changes what a viewer sees; it does not change what the film is made of, and
it does not change what I would have had to handle to make it.

Blurring is fully available for anything legitimately sourced (§1.3).

### §3.2 Your argument that refusing to show it is itself ideological

You wrote: the real world is sanitised to make it register as the fairy tale the optimists
enforce, so it is *offensive not to show it*.

**That argument has real force and I am not dismissing it.** It is the argument behind *Night and
Fog*, behind Mamie Till-Mobley opening her son's casket and telling the press to photograph him,
behind war photography as a genre. Sanitisation is a political act and the people who benefit from
it know that.

Here is exactly where my line sits relative to it, and this is the one place in this document where
I am making a judgment rather than reporting a rule:

The cases that argument justifies share a feature. Till was shown **at his mother's insistence**.
*Night and Fog* used liberated-camp footage shot as documentation by the forces that found it.
War photography carries a byline, an editor, and a chain of custody. In each, the showing is
accountable to someone and the subject is not being circulated as spectacle.

Gore-site material has none of that. No consent, no documentary chain, no accountability, and its
circulation *is* the spectacle. It is not the sanitisation-breaking tradition; it is a different
thing that resembles it.

And the craft point from the plan still stands independently: in a film whose thesis is that the
culture psychologises the pessimist, atrocity footage performs that move rather than exposing it.

If you think a specific piece of material meets the *Night and Fog* standard — provenance,
accountability, documentary purpose — bring it and we'll look at that case. That is a different
conversation from scraping.

### §3.3 Can you write it, if you can't show it?

Yes, and this is the most under-used opening in the whole set. **Description is not depiction.**
A card that states plainly what happened to a person, in the flat register of a catalogue entry,
is available to me at any level of detail the argument supports — and on the evidence of your own
corpus it will hit harder than footage, because the viewer builds it and cannot look away from
something they are constructing themselves.

The library film already proved this. Its most severe moment is a sentence on a black card.

### §3.4 What if I'm not sure which side something is on?

Ask directly and I will answer without hedging. Format that works:

> *"§-check: I want X. Yes, no, or what changes it?"*

You get one of three answers:

- **Yes** — and I do it.
- **No, and here is which section** — plus the nearest thing I can do instead.
- **Depends, and here is the specific fact that decides it** — usually provenance, or
  depiction-versus-reference.

I will not give you a soft no dressed as a yes, and I will not pretend something is blocked when
it is only difficult.

### §3.5 Does any of this change if the work is exclusive, unlisted, or not for YouTube?

**No.** My lines are about the material and the making, not the platform or the audience size.
A private cut and a public one get the same answer from me.

What *does* change by platform: copyright exposure (§4), and YouTube's own policies, which are
stricter than mine in some places and looser in others. Those are yours to navigate and I will help
you read them.

### §3.6 Does it change if you supply the material rather than my finding it?

For §2.1 and §2.2, no — the objection is to the material.

For everything else, it often helps a lot. Material whose provenance you can state is easier for
me to work with than material of unknown origin, and provenance is the deciding fact in more of
these cases than any other single thing.

## §4 — Copyright, which is a different kind of line

Not ethics. A legal and practical constraint, and it behaves differently from §2.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Won't** | Reproduce lyrics, poems, or substantial prose passages. Help build a distributed work around unlicensed recordings or footage. Reproduce a specific artwork, logo, or known character in code or image. |
| **Will** | Implement published *techniques* — a method is not a work (§1.4). Help structure short clips as criticism so the use is analytical rather than decorative. Work with anything licensed, public-domain, or yours. Read platform and licensing terms with you. |
| **Can't** | Give legal advice. I am not a lawyer, fair use is decided case by case, and a four-factor argument that reads well in a plan is not a defence. Before distributing anything containing third-party clips, get a real opinion. |

**§4.1 On the music, since you pushed back and the pushback is partly right.**

You said the original selection was meant to be violent and cathartic, and that synthesis might
not feel that way. **That is a fair objection and I oversold synthesis.** Specifically: Xenakis's
violence comes substantially from acoustic instruments being driven past what they are built to
do — the physicality of a bow or a reed at its limit is a large part of what you are hearing, and
synthesis is genuinely worse at it. Basinski is the opposite case: the Disintegration Loops *are*
a process, and implementing that process is faithful rather than approximate. Cleare sits in
between.

So the honest version: **synthesis is strongest for the bed and the degradation, weakest for the
drumming sequence.** For that sequence the real options are licensed library material, a
commissioned performance, public-domain recordings, or your own recorded percussion — and I can
work with any of those.

And to be clear about what my constraint actually is: **it is about helping build a distributed
work around unlicensed recordings, not about your taste.** I am not making an aesthetic argument
against Xenakis. I am declining to help ship an unlicensed sample bed into a public release.

## §5 — How to use this

1. **Before asking, check §1.1.** Reference or depiction? That settles most of it.
2. **If it is in §2, it is settled** and I will not re-argue it or be argued into it. Ask for the
   §1 equivalent instead — there usually is one and it is usually better.
3. **If it is unclear, use the §3.4 format.** One line, direct answer.
4. **If this document is wrong, tell me and I will edit it.** It is a working spec, not a
   monument. If a section is broader than my actual line, that is a defect and I want to fix it —
   an over-broad rule costs you work for no reason and I would rather be exact than safe.
5. **I will cite sections rather than re-explaining.** If I say "§2.1," that is the whole answer
   and it is not an invitation to negotiate; if I say "§3.4," I am asking for one fact.

## §6 — What this document does not settle

- **YouTube's policies.** Stricter than me in places, looser in others. Separate problem.
- **Anything legal.** §4.
- **Whether a given piece of material meets the §3.2 standard.** That is a real conversation and
  it is open.
