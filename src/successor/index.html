Mr. Grey is a scripted proxy — a projection of the writer's voice, run off a fixed record. Not the writer, and not a language model: a bounded bank of prepared answers and a rule for choosing one. The apparatus behind him is the Successor Protocol — a canon of one man's register plus the rule for speaking from it and never inventing past it. This page is the proxy's surface; the door, not the whole house.

The stage

The stage is the room itself — whichever companion holds the desk, at full size, the conversation floating over them. Same scripted matcher, same crisis floor; nothing you type leaves this browser. The Yūrei · Mr. Grey toggle at the head of the stage passes the desk live.

about · how it works · settings

The proxy

Mr. Grey’s black-cat avatar wakes into a slow regard, holds a listening stillness, and answers in his own register. He keeps a corner seat across the rest of the site; here, the stage is his.

What he will and won’t do

In this phase the proxy holds no positions. He answers for what the Protocol is and where the site points, and he deflects requests for stances — those belong to a later, ratified stage, not to the proxy’s say-so. Ask him what he is, whether he’s the author, or where to go, and he’ll tell you. Ask him what he thinks, and he’ll tell you plainly that a position isn’t his to hand out. When something falls outside the record, he says so rather than improvising a cover.

The one thing he never stylises

If you write about wanting to hurt yourself, the proxy drops the fiction and points you to a person. That floor is inherited byte-for-byte from the same safety code the rest of the site runs, and it fires before anything else he might say.

The desk companion

Mr. Grey is not the only voice here. Yūrei keeps the desk — the quiet companion who greets you across the rest of the site, waiting in a seat of her own until you call her. Like the proxy she is scripted: a fixed bank of prepared lines and a rule for choosing one. She is not a language model and she does not compose. She matches what you write against a set corpus and answers with the line that fits — or says plainly when nothing does.

She takes her name and her face from the yūrei mascot, the site’s small resident ghost. Where Mr. Grey is the writer’s proxy, bound to this page and the Successor Protocol, Yūrei is the house greeter — the default presence at the desk, everywhere. On this page the two share one stage: a small Yūrei · Mr. Grey toggle at its head passes the desk between them live, and whichever you are not speaking to waits behind the curtain. Two scripted companions, one apparatus, one floor — if either reads that you mean to harm yourself, both drop the script and point you to a person, inherited byte-for-byte from the rest of the site.

For the curious — how the desk works

Neither companion generates language. Each is a deterministic matcher: it scores what you type against a fixed corpus of prepared entries, takes the single best-scoring one, and returns it as written. No model, no network call, nothing learned between visits — the same input yields the same answer, and every answer was written in advance.

One rule sits above the matcher on both: the crisis floor. If your words read as self-harm it fires first — before any other entry can be chosen, and no matter which companion is active or set aside. The toggle on this page cannot gate it; it was never in that path.

And in this phase the proxy holds no positions. He answers only for what the Protocol is and where the site points; he never invents past the record. The stances of a later, ratified phase are written but unshipped — they wait on a deliberate act of ratification, not on the proxy’s say-so.

Agent settings

The desk has a sound and a texture — an ambient bed, small effects, and Yūrei’s optional voice. Tune them here. The panel writes to your browser only, and every page reads the same store.
