# "." — the Apparatus

A companion short to *Illogically Is*. 4:57.8.

**Photosensitivity and content warning.** One white frame and three full-field cuts to black; no strobe. The sound carries screams, crying, sirens and recorded voices in distress, and one whispered sentence at the end.


## The film, in numbers

- **Form** — one shot, one motion, one interruption
- **Duration** — 7140 frames at 24000/1001 = 297.798 s (4:57.8)
- **Picture** — 1920x1080, Blender/Cycles path tracing, 32 samples adaptive with denoising, AgX; 7019 frames rendered in 9.9 h on one laptop GPU
- **Clock** — the first frame is the dot at macro; the song's end section enters at frame 3073 (2:08.2); the interruption is frame 7019 (4:52.8); then one white frame, 48 frames of black, 48 frames of the first frame again, 24 of black
- **Determinism** — the picture is one script with a fixed seed and no keyframes: the same numbers make the same film

## The picture

- **The wall** — a plane of plaster displaced for real - perlin peaks, a voronoi bead, a trowel undulation, a micro tooth as bump - lit by a single point light that leaves the room as the room does.
- **The dot** — an 18 mm disc of black semi-gloss paint at eye height, 0.25 mm thick, with a meniscus at a wobbling rim, half-filling the stipple beneath it.
- **The pull-out** — 45 mm to 110 m along one axis, 35 mm to 14 mm of lens, a slow tilt to 18 degrees; the floor's 2.4 m slabs are the only fixed-size thing in the room - the scale the wall outgrows.
- **The grain** — the stipple's world scale grows with the pixel footprint (1x to 600x), so the plaster stays 6-8 px at every distance: the wall never stops being a wall seen close.
- **The dot, held** — past about 2:42 the painted dot is smaller than one pixel. It is held at a floor of 2.8 px, at the exact place the camera puts it, so that it never disappears.
- **The rumble** — from 4:00 the camera shakes, rising into the interruption, with a lurch on each blast. It is a rotation, and a rotation of a distant scene is a pure shift of the image: whole pixels, no resampling.

## The words

- **Surfacings** — 193 events drawn from the film's own material - its dream sentences, its Latin tags, its refrains - eroded by a three-octave plaster grain so that parts of every letter are missing. Nothing before 0:18, nothing after the interruption.
- **Flinches** — 30 of them, each one frame after a scream or a blast in the sound: the wall's light jumps 5-19 %.
- **The flood** — from 158.0 s the words stop being events and become a field - 671 of them, built from 565 whole sheets of phrases - until the wall is more text than plaster. It is clipped to the wall itself, so it never lies on the floor or the ceiling; it never enters a clear disc around the dot; and it drops out entirely for a quarter of a second before the third blast.
- **The reflection** — the floor is polished concrete, so it catches the words - inverted, smeared and gone within a metre of the wall's foot. The words themselves never touch it.

## The sound

- **The two songs** — see the table below; both are cited, neither is licensed.
- **The cascade** — 190 fragments of 19 recordings, thinned by a random process whose density, level, overlap, band, pan and treatment are curves over the span from the song's entry to the interruption. A governor holds any one source to a fraction of the span.
- **The radio** — 221 radio-frequency events synthesised on the clock - carriers, beating clusters, squeals, propagation static, tuning sweeps. Nothing sampled.
- **The whimper bed** — a surface from 0:40 - hiss, crackle, a slow wow - and crying on it from 0:55 to the interruption, under the music by about ten decibels. About a quarter of the crying is synthesised outright; the rest is whitened until the phonemes and the breath survive and the speaker does not.
- **The approach** — the song's end section does not arrive; it is approached for 28 seconds - its own first two seconds reversed through a long room, lowered five semitones and soured by a slow flanger, rising and resolving into true pitch at the join.
- **The screams that are no one's** — the scream at 4:41 and the roar at 4:49 are synthetic throats - a glottal line, the pushes of effort a body makes, period-doubling roughness, cracks and catches. Nothing in them is any recording's scream.
- **The closing sentence** — "I hate you", which no source says: whisper grains from two of the recordings, whitened until only air is left, then shaped by a formant score. It is spoken in the dark, and ends 0.2 s before the dot returns.
